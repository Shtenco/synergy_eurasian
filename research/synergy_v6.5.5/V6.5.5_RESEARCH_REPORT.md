# SYNERGY V6.5.5 — Self-Healing Economy
## Research report

### Executive result
Executed **2,400 policy paths**: 100 paired seeds × 4 policies in the severe regime, plus 500 paired seeds × 4 policies in the systemic regime. The network has 80 nodes, four sectors, 100 days, two supplier links per node, a prefunded 180-unit intervention pool, and a concentrated shock to the most downstream-critical nodes.

The network optimizer improves production recovery and external gross sales versus the liquidity-first policy, but it causes more average defaults. This is the central result: **maximizing restored production is not the same as minimizing insolvency**. A self-healing economy needs a joint objective over production, survival, and tail loss.

### What is actually demonstrated
1. Supplier rerouting plus prefunded repair can materially reduce production loss in this stylized network.
2. Network-critical restoration can shorten recovery time and slightly increase external gross sales and operating surplus.
3. A policy that repairs the most systemically important nodes can still sacrifice more individual firms than liquidity-first support.
4. The best policy depends on the loss function: survival, output, recovery speed, and tail-collapse exposure are different objectives.

### Model mechanics
- 80 nodes, 4 sectors, 20 nodes per sector; two supplier links per node.
- 100 days; shock starts on day 30.
- Severe: 18 high-dependency nodes disrupted for up to 25 days.
- Systemic: 24 high-dependency nodes disrupted for up to 38 days.
- Starting node cash: 40 units; intervention pool: 180 units.
- Repairing one disrupted node costs 18 pool units.
- Daily production depends on demand and the availability of the node's two suppliers.
- Revenue: 10 per production unit; variable cost: 5.5; fixed cost and debt service combined: 1.8 per live node/day.
- Supplier rerouting only changes future links. The current model does not include invoice-level settlement or supplier prices.
- Default occurs when node cash becomes negative after daily operating flows and any support.
- Recovery time is the first 5-day rolling mean at or above 90% of the pre-shock mean.
- Network importance is a damped multi-hop downstream dependency score; it is a heuristic, not a proven optimal centrality measure.

### Important limitations
This is a controlled synthetic experiment, not a calibrated forecast of real companies or a real-world probability of network collapse. Demand, costs, node cash, shock severity, supplier capacity, and repair costs are stylized assumptions. There is no endogenous price formation, inventory/lead-time accounting, household sector, payroll, tax, external financing market, or invoice-level double-entry ledger. The rescue policy is greedy and heuristic; it is not a globally optimal solution to a mixed-integer or stochastic control problem. The paired confidence intervals quantify seed-to-seed variation under this model only.

### V6.5.6 research direction
Replace separate repair and liquidity heuristics with a joint intervention allocator. For each candidate action, estimate:
- expected external production preserved over a finite horizon;
- probability of preventing node default;
- downstream cascade loss avoided;
- capital consumed and opportunity cost;
- contribution to CVaR95 of network loss.

Then compare a greedy policy, a rolling-horizon optimizer, and a robust CVaR-constrained allocator under the same paired shock paths. Add inventories, lead times, invoice-level cash settlement, supplier capacity limits, and explicit external demand before making economic claims.
