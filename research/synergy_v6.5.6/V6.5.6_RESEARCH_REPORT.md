# SYNERGY V6.5.6 — Joint Survival & Recovery Optimizer

## Experiment scope
Executed 1,600 paired policy paths: 100 seeds × 4 policies in severe regime and 300 seeds × 4 policies in systemic regime. Each path contains 80 nodes, four sectors, two suppliers per node, and 120 days. One common random seed per scenario is used across policies. The reserve is 180 synthetic units.

## Policies
- `liquidity_first`: rescue the lowest-cash nodes first; no active supplier rerouting.
- `network_greedy`: supplier rerouting and greedy ranking by estimated benefit per capital unit.
- `rolling_horizon`: supplier rerouting and discounted 5-day intervention scoring.
- `cvar_robust`: supplier rerouting, haircut to uncertain repair benefits, and concentration penalty. This is a robust heuristic, not an exact CVaR optimizer.

## Systemic regime results
See `systemic_policy_summary.csv` for exact calculated values. Values are averages over 300 paired seeds. Monetary values are synthetic model units. Gross sales are not net cash receipts.

## Methodology
The shock starts on day 35 and impairs the highest downstream-dependency nodes: 18 nodes in severe and 25 in systemic regime. Impairment lasts 22 or 36 days unless repaired. Each repair costs 18 reserve units and immediately restores one node. Liquidity support adds cash to a distressed live node, up to 8 units per action. Supplier rerouting changes future supplier links only. Operating cash flow is gross sales less variable costs and fixed/debt-service burden. A node defaults if its cash becomes negative after operations and interventions.

Recovery time is the first five-day rolling output average reaching 90% of the pre-shock average. `recovery_ratio` is mean output over days 90–119 divided by pre-shock mean output.

## Interpretation
The experiment compares intervention rules under identical paired shocks. No policy is guaranteed to dominate across survival, production, recovery speed, and tail losses. The correct policy depends on an explicit social/economic objective and the opportunity cost of reserve capital.

## Limitations
This is a synthetic, uncalibrated model. The candidate-action scores are heuristics, not exact marginal causal values. The rolling-horizon policy is a discounted ranking heuristic, not a solved multi-period optimization problem. The CVaR-labelled policy uses conservative score haircuts and concentration penalties; it does not optimize CVaR directly. There is no inventory/lead-time model, supplier pricing, invoice-level settlement, external financing, household sector, taxes, or calibrated real-world default process. Confidence intervals from this model describe variation across simulated seeds only.

## Required next validation
1. Rebuild the model with explicit inventory, lead times, and invoice-level double-entry cash settlement.
2. Estimate action values using paired counterfactual rollouts rather than proxy scores.
3. Solve a true rolling-horizon mixed-integer or stochastic optimization problem.
4. Calibrate shock and default parameters against real operational data.
5. Test repeated shocks and reserve replenishment/funding costs.
