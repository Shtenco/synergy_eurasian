# V6.5.4 decision note

The first measurable benefit appears under systemic stress, not moderate conditions.

The adaptive policy combines:
- supplier substitution for future orders after supplier failure;
- a prefunded liquidity pool;
- a separately tracked prefunded reserve.

In the 300-seed systemic paired sample, it reduced mean defaults by 1.92 nodes versus the isolated policy (95% CI 1.58 to 2.26). The sample frequency of a 40+ node collapse was 1.33% versus 3.00%. This is an exploratory result under synthetic assumptions, not a real-world probability.

The next model version should add:
1. invoice-level double-entry accounting and exact cash conservation;
2. persistent inventory, lead times and production bottlenecks;
3. endogenous supplier substitution capacity and prices;
4. sector-specific default dependence and wrong-way risk;
5. rescue prioritization based on external value preserved per unit of scarce capital;
6. capital opportunity cost and pool insolvency;
7. household payroll, taxes and external demand;
8. calibration against real payment and production data.

The critical research question is no longer simply "how much liquidity should the pool hold?" It is:
**which node should receive the next unit of scarce rescue capital to maximize the network's surviving external cash generation?**
