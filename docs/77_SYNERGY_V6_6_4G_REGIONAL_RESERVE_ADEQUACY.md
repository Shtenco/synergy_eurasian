# SYNERGY V6.6.4-G — regional reserve adequacy and sizing sensitivity

**Status:** paired reserve-size experiment implemented and added to CI.  
**Purpose:** measure the trade-off between guarantee capacity and the member liquidity absorbed to fund the reserve.

## 1. Reserve-size grid

`compare_regional_reserve_size_sensitivities(...)` evaluates a declared grid of reserve amounts per region. Each reserve level is replayed on the same seed-specific exogenous environment. Mutual-credit and hybrid modes are reported separately.

The experiment reports:
- initial reserve capital across both regions;
- mean guarantee payments and remaining reserve;
- reserve exhaustion rate and utilization;
- seller-side trade bad debt;
- service level and default counts;
- maximum cash-conservation residual.

The contribution scheme can be equal-member or production-capacity-weighted. An optional per-member opening-cash fraction cap uses the liquidity-capped allocator from V6.6.4-F. The experiment rejects a reserve level that cannot be funded within the declared contribution caps.

## 2. Economic interpretation

A larger reserve raises nominal guarantee capacity, but it also removes more opening liquidity from member cells. The net effect on production, service levels, defaults and seller losses is therefore not assumed to be monotonic. A reserve may remain underused while still imposing a material opportunity cost on members.

The reserve exhaustion rate is defined only for runs with a positive initial reserve and at least one guarantee payment: it is the fraction of runs in which the closing reserve is zero after a positive payout. Utilization is guarantee paid divided by initial reserve capital; it is zero when initial reserve capital is zero.

## 3. CI fixture

The CI CLI uses five reserve levels per region: 0, 5, 10, 20 and 40 synthetic currency units; three seeds; 20 days; severe synthetic regime; equal-member contributions; heterogeneous opening cash; and a 10% per-member contribution cap. It writes `outputs/synergy_v66_regional_reserve_sizing.json`.

This is a smoke-test grid, not an optimized reserve recommendation. A larger seed set and empirically calibrated loss process would be required before setting a real reserve target.

## 4. Boundaries

The model does not estimate real default probabilities, legal guarantee obligations, recovery rates, capital requirements, risk correlations or systemic importance. Reserve adequacy is measured only against the synthetic claims generated by the configured model. No real-world policy or regional reserve amount is recommended.

The sample illustrates the trade-off rather than a monotonic improvement. For mutual credit, increasing reserve from 5 to 10 units per region eliminated seller bad debt in this particular sample, but service level did not improve at that step. At 40 units per region, the reserve remained mostly unused and service level was lower than at 20, consistent with a larger amount of member cash being immobilized. In hybrid mode, substantial seller bad debt remained because the local guarantee does not cover cross-region exposures.

These are three-seed synthetic observations, not statistically stable estimates, not an optimized reserve level and not evidence that 10 units per region is suitable outside this fixture. Maximum cash-conservation residuals across the grid remained below 1.4×10⁻¹².


## 4. Absolute member cash floor

The reserve-size sensitivity accepts `minimum_post_contribution_cash` in addition to the optional fractional cap. Each tested reserve level must be funded without pushing any contributing cell below that nominal opening-cash floor. The CI fixture applies a 75-unit floor alongside the 10% contribution cap. This is a mechanical guardrail, not an estimate of operating liquidity or a recommended reserve level.
